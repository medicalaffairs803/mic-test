Why Check Your Mic Before A Discord Call?

Discord's own mic test lives inside Voice & Video settings and requires you to already be signed in. This page gives you a quick, no-login check of your input device before you even open the app — handy when you've just switched headsets or PCs.

It's also useful for spotting background noise problems, like a fan or keyboard clatter, before your teammates or friends point it out mid-call.

How To Test Your Mic Before Discord

1

Click "Start Mic Test" and allow microphone access in your browser.

2

Talk normally and check that the input level and waveform respond.

3

Check the noise level reading in a quiet room — high noise means Discord may need push-to-talk.

4

Open Discord and match the working input device under User Settings > Voice & Video.

Common Discord Microphone Issues

Input Sensitivity Too Low

Discord may cut off your voice if the sensitivity threshold is set too high.

Wrong Device Selected

Discord can default to the wrong microphone after plugging in a new headset.

Server-Muted

Some Discord servers mute new members by default — a working mic won't help until you're unmuted.

Background Noise Pickup

Sensitive condenser mics can pick up fans and typing without noise suppression enabled.

Discord Mic Test FAQs

Why is my mic not working on Discord but works elsewhere?

Discord has its own input device and sensitivity settings, separate from your OS. Confirm the mic works here first, then check Discord's Voice & Video settings.

What is push-to-talk and should I use it?

Push-to-talk only sends audio while a key is held, which helps if Discord picks up background noise. Useful if this test shows a high noise reading in a quiet room.

Why does my voice sound quiet in Discord voice chat?

Usually caused by low input volume or sensitivity set too high. Check your input level here, then adjust the mic volume slider in Discord to match.
